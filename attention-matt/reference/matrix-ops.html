Matrix ops for attention

Three operations, one shape rule, the two products attention is built from, and the ways they break.

The convention here

A matrix is an array of arrays; one row = one token. A shape is written (rows × columns): (T × d) is T tokens with d numbers each. When you read a table of numbers, the row is the thing doing the asking and the column is the thing being asked about.

[[1.5, -0.2, 0.7],      # (T × d) = (2 × 3): two tokens, three features each
 [0.1,  0.9, -0.4]]

The three operations

OperationRuleMeaning

a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + …		 similarity: positive when the vectors point the same way, negative when opposed. Attention's notion of "relevant" is this number.
matmul

transpose
transpose(A)		 (m × n) → (n × m)
A[i][j] lands at [j][i]		 swaps which axis is rows and which is columns, so the inner numbers of a product can be made to match.

The one rule

(m × n) · (n × p) → (m × p)

Inner numbers must match; outer numbers survive. The two inner counts must be equal because they are the length of the vectors being dotted — a violation is not a wrong answer, it is a product that does not exist. The two outer counts are what the answer is shaped like; the inner ones disappear into it.

A = [[1, 2, 3],          B = [[1, 0],          A · B = [[4,  5],
     [4, 5, 6]]               [0, 1],                   [10, 11]]
                              [1, 1]]
# (2 × 3) · (3 × 2) -> (2 × 2)
# cell (0,0) = row 0 of A · column 0 of B = [1,2,3] · [1,0,1] = 4

Why attention transposes K

Every key is a row of K. A single token's query is a row of Q. Matmul pairs rows of the left operand with columns of the right one, so to let one query reach every key in one product, the keys must be turned into columns:

Q · KT = (T × dk) · (dk × T) → (T × T)
weights · V = (T × T) · (T × dv) → (T × dv)
ProductShapeWhat entry (i, j) means
Q · KT — scores(T × T)how much token i's query likes token j's key
weights · V — output(T × dv)token i's new row: a weighted average of all of V's rows

The .T trap

A.T is right for a plain 2-D matrix. On a tensor with batch or head axes it reverses all axes — randn(2, 3, 4).T has shape (4, 3, 2), not (2, 4, 3) — and current PyTorch raises a deprecation warning telling you to use A.mT or transpose(-2, -1) instead. Attention code always writes transpose(-2, -1).

Same shape in and out, numbers unchanged, nothing ever mixeselement-wise multiply used where matmul was meantat least one output cell must combine all inner terms, not just the matching one
A (T × T) output where (T × dv) was expectedstopped after the scores; never multiplied by Vthe weights are intermediate — the output is weights · V

Sanity checks you can do by hand

  • Inner-match test: write the two shapes down, cancel the two inner numbers, and if what is left is the shape you got, you are right. (6 × 8) · (8 × 3) → (6 × 3).
  • Identity: A · I = A for a square A, where I has 1s on the diagonal and 0s elsewhere.
  • Double transpose: (AT)T = A, exactly.
  • Non-commutativity: A · B and B · A generally have different shapes and different entries; sometimes only one of the two exists.
  • Rebracketing is safe: (A · B) · C = A · (B · C) — which is why attention can be chained at all.
  • Batch axes pass through: (2, 4, 8) @ (2, 8, 4) → (2, 4, 4); the rule above still only looks at the last two axes.
